10 Common Massage Questions Answered in Redmond, WA

Clear, practical answers about clothing, pressure, soreness, timing, safety and what to expect from a professional massage in Redmond.

Quick answer: You control your clothing, pressure, conversation and focus areas during a massage. Share relevant health information, speak up whenever something feels uncomfortable and ask a healthcare professional when you are unsure whether massage is appropriate for you.

People considering a massage often have the same practical questions: What should I wear? Will deep pressure work better? Is soreness normal? How frequently should I book? These questions matter because a good massage is not a test of endurance. It should be a collaborative appointment shaped around your comfort, goals and health history.

Below are ten of the questions clients ask most often. The answers reflect professional appointment expectations and current safety guidance from established health resources. They are general information, not a diagnosis or a substitute for care from your healthcare provider.

1. What should I wear to a massage?

Wear clean, comfortable clothing that is easy to change in and out of. During the session, undress only to your personal comfort level. A professional massage should use appropriate draping, uncovering only the area being worked on while the rest of your body remains covered.

If you prefer to stay fully clothed, say so when booking or before the session begins. Some focused or compression-based work can be adapted for clothing. Your therapist can explain what is practical for the service you selected.

2. Do I need to shower, shave or prepare my body?

Normal personal hygiene is enough. You do not need to shave, change your body or feel self-conscious about scars, body hair or skin texture. Arrive as you are, while mentioning any open wounds, contagious skin conditions, fresh tattoos, burns or irritated areas that should be avoided.

Remove complicated jewelry when practical. If you have allergies or fragrance sensitivities, ask about the oils, lotions or aromatherapy products that may be used.

3. Should a massage hurt to be effective?

No. Massage does not need to cause severe pain to be worthwhile. Firm or focused work can feel intense, but sharp pain, burning, numbness, tingling or pressure that makes you hold your breath and brace are reasons to speak up immediately.

The Mayo Clinic's massage overview advises clients to request lighter pressure when anything feels too strong. More pressure is not automatically better; the useful level is the one your body can comfortably tolerate.

4. What is the difference between Swedish and deep tissue massage?

Swedish massage generally emphasizes flowing movements and relaxation, with pressure adjusted to your preference. Deep tissue massage usually uses slower, more focused work around persistent areas of tension.

The service name matters less than communicating your goal. You can want relaxation with medium pressure or focused work without extremely deep pressure. Read our complete Swedish versus deep tissue comparison before choosing.

5. How long should my massage be?

A 30-minute appointment can work well for one limited focus area. Sixty minutes suits many standard sessions. Ninety minutes allows more time for full-body massage plus focused attention to specific areas. A two-hour appointment may suit clients who prefer a slower pace or several priorities, when that service is available.

Longer is not always necessary. Choose based on your goal, schedule, comfort and budget. Review the current massage lengths and prices before booking.

6. How often should I get a massage?

There is no universal schedule. Some people book occasionally for relaxation, while others choose a regular interval that fits their goals and budget. Your response to the previous session, activity level, health history and reason for booking all matter.

Avoid promises that a fixed number of sessions will solve a condition. Reassess how you feel, discuss realistic goals with your massage therapist and involve a healthcare provider when symptoms are persistent, worsening or unexplained.

7. Is soreness after a massage normal?

Some people notice temporary tenderness after firm or focused work, while others do not. Severe pain, significant bruising, new numbness, weakness, swelling or symptoms that concern you should not be dismissed as a normal requirement of massage.

Tell your therapist how you responded before the next session so pressure and technique can be adjusted. Seek appropriate medical guidance when symptoms are severe, unusual or do not improve.

8. What should I do before and after my appointment?

Before your appointment, avoid arriving immediately after a very heavy meal if lying down would feel uncomfortable. Allow enough time to find the location and complete any intake information without rushing. Use the restroom, silence your phone and think about the areas you want addressed or avoided.

Afterward, take your time sitting up and getting dressed. Resume your normal hydration routine rather than forcing excessive water. Follow any service-specific guidance and pay attention to how your body responds.

9. Do I have to talk during the massage?

No. You can request a quiet appointment. You should still speak whenever something affects safety or comfort: pressure, temperature, positioning, draping, music, fragrance or an area you no longer want massaged.

You may change your mind during the session. Consent is ongoing, and clear feedback helps the therapist personalize the appointment.

10. When should I ask a healthcare provider before getting a massage?

Massage is generally considered low risk when performed appropriately by a trained professional, but it is not risk-free for everyone. The National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health recommends discussing concerns with a healthcare provider and sharing relevant medical conditions and treatments with the massage therapist.

Ask for medical guidance when you have a recent injury or surgery, a known or suspected blood clot, a bleeding disorder or blood-thinning medication, severe osteoporosis, healing wounds, burns, fractures, significant nerve damage, pregnancy-related concerns or another condition requiring special precautions. Do not use massage to delay evaluation of new, severe or unexplained symptoms.

What information should I share before the session?

  • Your main goal: relaxation, general muscle tension or a specific focus area.
  • Your preferred pressure and any previous response to massage.
  • Areas you want prioritized and areas you want avoided.
  • Relevant injuries, surgeries, pregnancy, allergies, medications or health conditions.
  • Positioning needs, such as difficulty lying face down or on one side.
  • Whether you prefer quiet or occasional check-ins.
